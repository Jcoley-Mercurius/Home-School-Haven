# MPS Scope & Prioritization Standard v1.0

## Scope hierarchy

Use:

**PRODUCT → CAPABILITY → FEATURE → REQUIREMENT → ACCEPTANCE**

Do not treat a screen, component, technology, or vendor as a product capability unless the product value depends on it.

## Release boundaries

For each release define:

- goal and target users
- included capabilities/features
- explicit exclusions
- dependencies and assumptions
- launch/validation criteria
- migration or communication needs

Use MVP to mean the smallest coherent product that can deliver and test the intended value—not the smallest amount of code.

Do not call scope an MVP or release-ready definition while high-impact product policy or foundational user/outcome constraints remain unresolved. Early scope must remain provisional.

## Prioritization

Use a simple method appropriate to evidence:

- `must | should | could | wont_this_release`
- `now | next | later`
- evidence-based scoring only when input data exists

Evaluate user value, business value, risk reduction, learning value, urgency, dependencies, effort/complexity, and reversibility. Expose uncertainty and tradeoffs.

Do not use arithmetic to disguise subjective assumptions. Approval belongs to the accountable decision owner.

## Scope control

Every newly proposed feature must state the user/problem/outcome, release impact, dependencies, acceptance impact, and what moves out if capacity is constrained.

Record out-of-scope decisions so they do not repeatedly re-enter discovery.
