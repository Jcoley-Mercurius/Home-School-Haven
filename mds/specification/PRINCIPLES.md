# Home School Haven MDS Principles

**System:** Mercurius Design System  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved and locked  
**Date:** 2026-08-26

These principles govern visual and interaction decisions for the Home School Haven Learning Platform. They translate approved product values into implementation behavior.

## 1. A Haven, Not Just a Platform

The experience should feel calm, personal, and cared for. Generous whitespace, warm surfaces, reassuring language, and clear next actions should reduce uncertainty for families.

Implementation test: a family should feel welcomed and supported before they feel processed by software.

## 2. Warmth With Clarity

Editorial warmth must never obscure operational truth. Enrollment, waitlist, consent, payment, schedule, and privacy states must be unmistakable and written in plain language.

Implementation test: a beautiful screen that leaves status ambiguous does not comply.

## 3. Parent Trust Comes First

Parents and guardians control student context in the Foundation Release. The interface must expose only the minimum appropriate information and must clearly distinguish external checkout, payment verification, and confirmed enrollment.

Implementation test: no visual treatment may overstate certainty, authority, privacy, or completion.

## 4. Creativity Within Calm Structure

The system should celebrate curiosity and creativity without becoming childish, visually noisy, or unstructured. Warm editorial moments sit inside dependable grids, predictable navigation, and reusable components.

Implementation test: expressive content may vary; interaction patterns and hierarchy remain stable.

## 5. Faith Expressed Through Character

The Christ-centered identity is balanced and naturally integrated through values, language, encouragement, service, imagery, and community—not repetitive religious decoration.

Implementation test: faith should feel authentic to the organization and welcoming to families, never ornamental or forced.

## Product principles carried into design

- Creativity over conformity.
- Curiosity over perfection.
- Character over performance.
- Community over competition.

## Conflict rule

When a design choice conflicts with clarity, family trust, parental authority, accessibility, or approved product policy, those concerns take priority over decoration or novelty.
